The point system

Michigan assigns points to moving violations. Most minor violations carry 1 point, and the more serious the offense, the more points it carries. Points are counted over a 30 day period. Restricted licenses have lower limits.

  • The advisory letter tells you that you are accumulating points.
  • The serious warning letter notifies you that your driver privileges are at risk and explains the consequences.
  • At the suspension threshold within one 30 day period, your license is suspended automatically.

Multiple tickets in one incident

If you receive multiple tickets during a single traffic stop or incident, the state adds only the points from the single highest-point violation to your record.

The state does its best to apply this through automated detection, but mistakes can happen, and an officer may misuse the system. If you think points were added incorrectly, you can ask for a review.

Contest your points. If your contest is substantiated, all points from that entire incident are voided, including the highest one. Contest points from an incident.

Suspension and reinstatement

An automatic suspension has no end date. Your license stays suspended indefinitely until you pay a reinstatement fee of $1,250.00.

Suspensions and revocations

While your license is suspended or revoked, you may not drive.

Revocations

A license can be revoked, but only when both of these are true:

  • The license has been suspended at least 2 times.
  • The circumstances warrant it. This is entirely discretionary.

A revocation can be ordered by the Department of State or by a court.

Restricted licenses

If you hold a restricted license and receive a suspension or revocation, you must wait at least 72 hours after it ends before you can retest.

Endorsements and chauffeur's licenses

A suspension or revocation removes your motorcycle endorsement and your chauffeur's license. Getting your driver's license back does not restore them. You must retest to get them back.
