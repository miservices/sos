What the Bureau does

The Bureau of Administrative Hearings and Appeals gives people a way to challenge a decision made about them. Appeals are heard before an Administrative Law Judge. The Bureau hears appeals of decisions by state agencies, counties, and municipalities.

How an appeal works

  1. Sign in to SOS e-ServicesOpen Appeals and Contests.
  2. File your appeal or contestSay which decision you are challenging and why.
  3. The Bureau reviews itYour request is opened as a case. A hearing officer takes it up and may add notes.
  4. A hearing is scheduledIf your case needs a hearing, it is scheduled before an Administrative Law Judge.
  5. You get a decisionThe case is closed with a result. You can follow it in e-Services.

Case statuses

StatusWhat it means
OpenYour appeal has been filed and is waiting for review.
In reviewThe Bureau is reviewing your appeal.
ScheduledA hearing has been set before an Administrative Law Judge.
ClosedA decision has been made. The result is shown with it.

Possible results

ResultWhat it means
UpheldThe original decision stands.
OverturnedThe original decision is reversed.
DismissedThe appeal was dismissed without a decision on the original ruling.
WithdrawnThe appeal was withdrawn by the person who filed it.

Good to know

  • Every case is recorded on your account, so you can check where it stands at any time.
  • Appeals about driving points and suspensions can also be started from the contest page.
  • Decisions about courts and attorneys are handled by the Michigan Courts, not here.
