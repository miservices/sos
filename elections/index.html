The election calendar

Every general election in Michigan, state, county, and city, runs on the same calendar. Every office filled in a general election serves a one year term, January 1 to December 31.

Online voting is available through the Secretary of State's online system for the state election, and a Secretary of State approved system for county and city elections. Read more about absentee voting, or find your polling place.

Who can vote

Michigan citizens and residents are registered to vote automatically. There is no form to file. Learn about voter registration or check your status.

Ranked preference voting

Four races fill more than one seat at once. For those, instead of picking a single candidate, you rank as many as you would like, up to the number of open seats. Your top choice gets the highest number of points for that race, your second choice the next highest, and so on. Points are awarded per ballot and added up across every voter. The winners are whoever earns the highest total preference score, not whoever gets the most first choice votes.

You do not have to rank every open seat. But the point values do not change based on how many candidates you rank. If you only rank your top choice, that candidate still receives the full top point value from your ballot.

Ties and runoffs

Most ties are settled by voters in a runoff. Online voting for a runoff runs January 1 to January 10 of the term year, with in person voting on January 5.

The exception is a tie for the top preference score in the Legislature, Judiciary, Board of Commissioners, or City Council race. That tie determines who becomes Speaker, Chief Justice, Chairperson, or President of the Council, so it is not sent to a runoff. It is broken by the newly elected Governor, except for a City Council President tie, which is broken by the newly elected Mayor.

Special elections

When a seat becomes vacant outside the regular cycle, a special election fills the remainder of the term. It has the same structure as a general election, a period of online voting followed by an in person Election Day, on a timeline set by the Secretary of State for state offices, or by the County Clerk for county and city offices.

The one exception is the Governor's office. If it becomes vacant, the Lieutenant Governor succeeds to the office directly, with no special election.
