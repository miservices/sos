Starting and keeping a business in Michigan

The Division of Business and Occupational Licensing handles business registrations for the Michigan Secretary of State. A registered business is on the public record, can use its name, and, depending on its type, can protect its owners from personal responsibility for its debts.

Fees are charged when your application is approved. Sole proprietorships and general partnerships are the simplest to start, but the owners are personally responsible. An LLC or corporation separates the business from its owners and has to file an annual report to stay active.

Business status

StatusWhat it means
ActiveThe business is registered and, where an annual report is required, it is up to date.
Past dueThe annual report was not filed by its due date. A late fee applies and the business must file to return to active.
Administratively dissolvedThe report stayed unfiled past the grace period. The business loses its good standing and its name can be taken by someone else.
DissolvedThe owners closed the business.

What registering does and does not do

  • It puts your business name and owners on the public record.
  • It reserves your name against another business of the same type registering it.
  • It does not license any profession. Doctors, paramedics, EMTs and bail bond agents need a professional license as well.
  • It does not make you a notary. Notary commissions are handled through the Great Seal pages.

Where to file

Registrations and annual reports are filed online in SOS e-Services with your MiGOVT account. Every filing is reviewed by the Division, and you can follow it in e-Services.

Business Services are for people with a Secretary of State account. Your PIN is the owner of record on every filing you make.
