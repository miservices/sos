Term lengthJanuary 1 to December 31Every office filled in a general election serves a one-year term.
Election Day21 days before the term begins. In practice, around December 11 of the prior year.
Online voting window30 to 10 days before the term begins. Available through the Secretary of State's online system for the state election, and a Secretary of State approved system for county and city elections.

Ranked preference voting

Four races fill more than one seat at once. For those, instead of picking a single candidate, you rank as many as you would like, up to the number of open seats.

How the scoring works

Your top choice gets the highest number of points for that race, your second choice the next highest, and so on. Points are awarded per ballot and added up across every voter. The winners are whoever earns the highest total preference score, not whoever gets the most first choice votes.

You do not have to rank every seat

You do not have to rank every open seat. But the point values themselves do not change based on how many candidates you rank. If you only rank your top choice, that candidate still receives the full top point value from your ballot.

Ties, runoffs and head positions

Most ties are settled by voters in a runoff. A handful of specific ties are settled by the state's newly elected leadership instead.

Runoff elections

If a race ends in a tie, a runoff election is held to settle it.

The exception: head positions

A tie for the top preference score in the Legislature, Judiciary, Board of Commissioners, or City Council race is not sent to a runoff, because that tie determines who becomes Speaker of the Legislature, Chief Justice, Chairperson, or President of the Council.

Those specific ties are broken by the newly elected Governor, except for a City Council President tie, which is broken by the newly elected Mayor.

Special elections

General elections happen on a fixed yearly cycle, but seats sometimes come open in between.

Filling a vacancy

When a seat becomes vacant outside the regular election cycle, a special election is held to fill the remainder of the term. It follows the same basic structure as a general election, a period of online voting followed by an in-person Election Day, on a timeline set by the Secretary of State for state offices, or by the County Clerk for county and city offices.

The Governor's office

The one exception is the Governor's office: if it becomes vacant, the Lieutenant Governor succeeds to the office directly, without a special election.
