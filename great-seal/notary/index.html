About notaries

The notary program is inactive. Michigan law, specifically 2026 PA 17, says notaries are not needed.

Notary services
The notary program is inactive. Under Michigan law, specifically 2026 PA 17, notaries are not needed. The Office is not accepting notary commission applications or renewals.

Why the program is inactive

As of now, Michigan law, specifically 2026 PA 17, says notaries are not needed. A document does not have to be notarized to be valid, so the Office of the Great Seal is not commissioning notaries. The notary program is inactive.

QuestionAnswer
Can I apply to be a notary?No. New applications are closed.
Can I renew a commission?No. Renewals are closed.
Do I need a document notarized?No. Under 2026 PA 17 notarization is not needed.
What about my existing commission?It stays on record until it expires. You can resign it in Notarial Services.

What a notary public does

A notary public is an official witness. A notary checks who a person is, watches them sign an important document, and adds a stamp and signature to show it was done properly. People trust a notarized document because the notary is commissioned by the state.

Notarial actWhat the notary does
AcknowledgmentConfirms the signer is who they say they are and that they signed the document willingly.
Oath or affirmationHas the person swear or affirm that what they say is true.
JuratWatches the person sign and swear to a document, then certifies it.
Signature witnessingWatches a person sign a document and certifies that they did.

What a notary cannot do

  • Notarize a document if the signer is not there in person.
  • Notarize a document the notary has a personal interest in, or one they signed themselves.
  • Give legal advice or explain what a document means.
  • Notarize with a commission that is expired, suspended, revoked, or resigned.
  • Notarize a document that is blank or that the signer does not understand.

Requirements (while the program was open)

RequirementDetails
AgeAt least 18 years old
ResidencyA citizen or resident of Michigan. You are commissioned for the county you live in.
OathYou swear to perform the duties faithfully and follow the law
Fee$20.00, charged only if you are approved
Term4 years from the day you are approved

Your commission

When you are approved you receive a commission number, written like NP-482913, and your commission is recorded under your PIN. Your stamp shows the words Notary Public and State of Michigan, your county, your commission number, and the date your commission expires.

StatusWhat it means
ActiveYou can notarize.
ExpiredYour 4 years ended. You cannot notarize until you apply for a new commission.
SuspendedThe Office paused your commission while it looks into a complaint. You cannot notarize.
RevokedThe Office ended your commission for misconduct.
ResignedYou ended your commission yourself.

Complaints and discipline

The Office of the Great Seal, part of the Bureau of Elections, can suspend or revoke a commission if a notary breaks the law, notarizes without the signer present, or lies on a filing. A notary who is disciplined is told in SOS e-Services.

Questions

Will the program come back? The Office will post here if the law changes.

Do I need a business to be a notary? No. A commission belongs to you as a person. It is separate from a business registration.

Can I notarize in any county? Your commission is recorded for the county you live in.

How do I start? See Notary services.
